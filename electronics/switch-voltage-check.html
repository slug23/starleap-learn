Switch Voltage Check

The idea

A build can look right and still be wrong. The meter doesn't look — it measures. A working transistor switch has only two states, told on and told off, and every row sits at a voltage you can know ahead of time in each. Measure the rows in order and stop at the first one that disagrees: the bug is touching that row.

Before you start

  • The Debugging Loop comes first. Power, trace, nodes, one-way parts — and the transistor is a one-way part: flat face toward you, E · B · C. This card is for the build that passes the loop and still won't obey.
  • Meter on V⎓. Black probe on a wire stub in the ground rail, and it stays there. The red probe does the visiting: touch it to a component leg or a bare wire stub in the row.
  • Read the unit. 150 mV is 0.15 V — nearly zero, not one hundred fifty.
  • Park the control wire's free end in a rail rather than holding it — you'll need both hands for the probes. Write every reading in the Yours column, good or bad. Checks in order: each one assumes the checks before it passed.

Wthe + rail's numberThe control wire carries the rail's voltage to the 4.7 kΩ.

If a reading disagrees

You readLook at
W low, or a number that driftsThe control wire: is its free end really in the + rail? Is its other end in the 4.7 kΩ's row? Beep-test the jumper itself.
B near 5 VNo current is entering the base. Is the transistor's B leg in this row? Is its E leg reaching ground? Flat face toward you, E · B · C? If all three are right, suspect the transistor itself.
B near 0 VNothing is feeding the base. The 4.7 kΩ: one leg in W, the other in B? Or something else in the base row is tying it to ground.
B is right, but C is above 0.3 V and the LED is dim or darkThe base is asking and the transistor isn't delivering. Transistor in backwards — C and E trading places — or a base resistor that isn't 4.7 kΩ. Read its stripes: yellow · violet · red.
C near 0 V, LED darkThe switch is on, but nothing is arriving to switch. The break is above it: LED backwards, both LED legs in one row, or a leg a row off. M tells you which side of the middle row the break is on.

3 Told off

Control wire's free end in the ground rail. The LED should be fully dark.

RowExpectWhyYours
W0 VThe control wire now carries ground.
B0 VNothing feeds the base, so nothing lifts it.
C3.0 – 3.7 VSwitch open, so the collector rises — but not to 5: a dark red LED still holds about a volt and a half.
The LED — with your eyesfully dark, no glowAn open switch, an open loop.

If a reading disagrees

You seeLook at
LED still lit, B at 0 V, C under 0.3 VThe transistor is off, so the collector row has some other way to ground. What else is plugged into it — a jumper, a neighbor's leg? If nothing, suspect the transistor.
LED still lit, B near 0.7 VSomething besides the 4.7 kΩ is feeding the base. Trace every wire that lands in the base row.
C near 5 VNothing in the collector loop is taking its share: the LED is being skipped. Are both of its legs in one row?

Both states match? The switch is cleared. If you're on a Part 2 build, carry on below.

Still stuck? Bring the numbers

A filled-in card is a bug report. "Rails good. Told on, the base reads 0.7 but the collector reads 1.8, and the LED's dim" gets you fast help — and usually you'll see the bug yourself while you're writing the sentence.
