Sensor Voltage Check

The idea

A build can look right and still be wrong. The meter doesn't look — it measures. The night-light is two stages joined at one row, so test it the way it's built: each stage alone, then the two joined. Every row has a voltage you can know ahead of time. Measure in order and stop at the first reading that disagrees: the bug is touching that row.

Before you start

  • The Debugging Loop comes first. Power, trace, nodes, one-way parts — the transistor's flat face toward you, E · B · C. (The LDR is the one part here with no direction.) This card is for the build that passes the loop and still won't sense.
  • Meter on V⎓ — not Ω, where section 4 left it. Black probe on a wire stub in the ground rail, and it stays there. The red probe does the visiting: touch it to a component leg or a bare wire stub in the row.
  • Read the unit. 450 mV is 0.45 V.
  • "Room light" means the disc face up, nothing over it. "Cupped" means both hands sealed over it — a partner cups while you measure. Write every reading in the Yours column, good or bad. Checks in order: each one assumes the checks before it passed.

Three healthy surprises

The cupped tap stalls under 1 V. Once the base conducts, it holds the tap down near its own 0.7 V, and the divider's extra push goes into the base as current instead. Nothing is broken. In room light, B matches T. No current flows through the 4.7 kΩ, so nothing is lost across it. Cupped, C may not reach 0.3 V. The dark only feeds the base a little current, so a weaker transistor turns only partway on — the LED is lit but not full, and a neighbor's may be brighter.

If a reading disagrees

You seeLook at
Cupped, T climbs past 2 V and the LED stays darkThe tap's command isn't reaching the base. Compare W with T: different numbers mean the control wire isn't joining them. The same number means look at the 4.7 kΩ between W and B.
Room light: the LED glows and T reads 0.55 V or moreLook back at check 2's room-light reading — it was already too high before the switch joined. That table has the suspects.
Room light: T under 0.5 V, but the LED glowsSomething besides the tap is feeding the base. Trace every wire that lands in the base row.
Your own shadow trips itThat's not a bug — the sensor is working. Angle the board toward the light.

Still stuck? Bring the numbers

A filled-in card is a bug report. "Both stages pass alone. Joined and cupped, the tap reads 3.1 V, and the 4.7 kΩ's far row reads 0" gets you fast help — and usually you'll see the bug yourself while you're writing the sentence.
